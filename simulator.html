Link to biotechnology

Interferometry turns tiny optical path differences into visible fringe shifts. A transparent film of thickness t and index nf placed in one beam shifts the pattern by N = (nf − 1)t / λ fringes, so very small thicknesses can be read from the shift. The slider uses nf = 1.38, typical of biological material.

The same principle drives optical coherence tomography (OCT), which images retinal and tissue layers by interfering light reflected from different depths, and label-free biosensors and phase microscopy.
